About

An exploration, not a prediction.

If AI dramatically increases humanity's productive capability, how could we use that capability so that every person can reach the basics of life — and have more freedom to pursue a life they actually value?

This is an idea deck. It presents possible mechanisms: systems, technologies, policies, businesses, infrastructure concepts, and experiments that could contribute to that goal. It does not claim that AI will solve poverty, end scarcity, or produce universal prosperity. Nothing here is inevitable.

Technology does not determine outcomes

What actually happens depends on economics, infrastructure, institutions, politics, culture, human behaviour, physical resources, and governance. A perfectly good technical idea can fail completely for reasons that have nothing to do with technology — and a mediocre one can succeed because the politics aligned.

Why every idea has a reality check

Optimism is cheap and mostly useless. Each idea here lists what could go wrong, who could be harmed, what resources are required, and what assumptions must hold. An idea that cannot survive scrutiny is not worth the attention, however beautiful it sounds.

On AI controlling important things

Several ideas put AI in a coordination role over food, water, energy, housing or public money. Those ideas deserve extra suspicion, not less. Concentrated control of essential systems — by a company, a state, or an algorithm — is a risk that can outweigh the efficiency it buys. Where that risk appears, it is named.

How to use this

Swipe right to keep an idea, left to pass, up to read it properly, down to go back. Everything is stored locally in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. The deck works offline.

What this is not

Not a social network. Not a campaign. Not a fundraising platform. Not a chatbot. Not a prediction engine. Not a claim that abundance is coming. It is a way to hold more possibilities in your head at once than you had before you opened it.

The question underneath everything

How could we use artificial intelligence to reduce the amount of human effort and economic scarcity required to secure the basic necessities of life — while increasing people's freedom to pursue lives they actually value? There is no single answer here. There are only better and worse experiments.
